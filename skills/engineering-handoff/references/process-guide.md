# Process guide

Typical starting values for design-intent packages. They are rules of thumb to start a conversation with an engineer or supplier; every value is "to confirm".

## Choosing a process by quantity

| Quantity | Plastics | Metals | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 to 50 | 3D printing (FDM, SLA, SLS, MJF), CNC from block | CNC machining, laser cut and bent sheet | No tooling; highest cost per part |
| 50 to 2,000 | Urethane casting, low-volume aluminium tooling | CNC, sheet metal, investment casting | Soft tooling |
| 2,000 and up | Injection moulding | Die-casting, stamping, extrusion, forging | Tooling cost pays back over volume |

## Injection-moulded plastics

- **Wall thickness:** usually 1.5 to 3 mm, kept as even as possible; ribs about 50 to 60% of the wall they support.
- **Draft:** at least 1° on smooth faces, about 1° extra per 0.025 mm of texture depth; textured faces often 3° or more.
- **Radii:** inside corners at least half the wall thickness; outside radius equals inside radius plus wall.
- **Parting line:** place it on an edge or a change of surface, away from hero faces.
- **Undercuts:** avoid, or plan for side actions, lifters or snap-fit flex.
- **Common materials:** ABS and PC-ABS for housings, PP for living hinges and food containers, PA (nylon) for strength, PC for clear parts, TPE for soft-touch overmoulds.
- **Finishes:** polished (SPI A), textured (VDI 3400 references, for example VDI 24 to 33), in-mould decoration, paint, soft-touch coating.

## Metals

- **CNC aluminium (6061, 6063):** very crisp detail; anodised finishes (clear, black, colours); bead-blast before anodising for satin.
- **Die-cast aluminium or zinc:** complex shapes at volume; needs draft (1 to 2°) and even walls; usually painted or powder-coated.
- **Sheet metal (steel, aluminium):** bend radius at least the sheet thickness; keep holes at least two thicknesses from bends.
- **Extrusion (aluminium):** constant profile, cut to length; ideal for frames, rails and handles.
- **Stainless steel:** brushed (No. 4), mirror (No. 8) or bead-blasted; harder to machine, durable for kitchen and outdoor.

## Other materials

- **Glass:** tempered for safety; bends and complex curves add cost.
- **Ceramics:** about 10 to 15% shrinkage when fired; tolerances are loose.
- **Wood:** grain direction matters for strength; finish with oil, lacquer or wax; moves with humidity.
- **Textiles and leather:** specify weight, backing and stitching; check abrasion and lightfastness.

## Vehicles and large products

- **Body panels:** stamped steel or aluminium at volume; composites (SMC, carbon fibre) for low volume or complex shapes; thermoformed plastics for shuttles and low-speed vehicles.
- **Panel gaps:** typical car shut lines are about 3 to 5 mm; flush glazing and tight gaps add cost.
- **Lighting:** lamp graphics are set by optics and regulations (position, height, colour); check early.
- **Two-wheelers:** hydroformed or welded aluminium or steel frames; battery packs need ventilation, protection and service access.
- **Rail, aircraft and boats:** fire, smoke and toxicity rules govern interior materials; weight is critical; involve certification specialists from the start.

## Compliance topics (to confirm with specialists)

- Electrical products: electrical safety, electromagnetic compatibility, radio, hazardous substances, battery transport.
- Food contact: material migration rules for the target market.
- Children's products: toy safety, small parts, coatings.
- Vehicles: type approval or homologation, lighting, crash, emissions or battery safety.
- Everything: product safety, labelling, packaging and take-back or recycling rules.
