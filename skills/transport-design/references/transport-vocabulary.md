# Transport design vocabulary

Use the term in render prompts, where it helps the image model, and the plain meaning when talking to people.

## Proportion and package

| Term | Plain meaning | Prompt tip |
|---|---|---|
| Package | Where people, batteries or engine, luggage and wheels sit | Describe seats and layout before styling |
| Wheelbase | Distance between front and rear axles | Give it as a share of overall length ("wheelbase about 70% of length") |
| Overhang | Body that sticks out past the wheels, front and rear | "Very short overhangs" reads modern and agile |
| Dash-to-axle | Distance from the windscreen base to the front axle | Long dash-to-axle reads premium, rear-drive and powerful |
| Cab-forward | Cabin pushed toward the front | Typical of electric cars, vans and buses |
| Greenhouse / DLO | The glass area; DLO is the daylight opening, the side-window shape | "Low, tapering greenhouse", "DLO with a kick-up at the rear" |
| Body-to-glass ratio | Height of body side against height of glass | Sporty is about 2:1, practical is closer to 1.5:1 |
| Stance | How the vehicle sits: wheels at the corners, track width, ride height | "Wide track, wheels flush to the arches, planted stance" |
| Rake | How far a windscreen, A-pillar or fork is laid back from vertical; for a whole vehicle, a nose-down, tail-up attitude | "Steeply raked windscreen" |
| Tumblehome | How much the side glass leans inward toward the roof | Strong tumblehome shows wide shoulders |

## Surfaces and lines

| Term | Plain meaning | Prompt tip |
|---|---|---|
| Shoulder line | The highest horizontal line running along the body side | "Strong shoulder line over the rear wheel" |
| Beltline | Line where body side meets glass | "Rising beltline", "beltline kick at the C-pillar" |
| Feature line / character line | A crisp crease along the body | Use one or two, not many |
| A-, B-, C-, D-pillar | Roof supports, front to back | "Blacked-out B-pillar for a floating roof" |
| Floating roof | Roof that seems to hover because a pillar is hidden in black | Common premium cue |
| Surface tension | Panels that look taut, as if inflated | "Taut, full surfaces with soft highlights" |
| Highlight / reflection line | How light runs along the body | Use the vehicle studio lighting so lines read |
| Graphic | Shapes of lamps, grille and glass seen as flat shapes | "Single full-width light bar graphic" |

## Two-wheelers

| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| Rake and trail | Rake is the fork's angle from vertical; trail is the distance from where the steering axis meets the road to the front wheel's contact patch, which trails behind that point. Together they set how the bike steers and looks |
| Triangle | The space between frame tubes; designers fill or open it |
| Tank and seat line | The line from tank to tail; flat for café racers, rising for sport bikes |
| Down tube | Lower frame tube; on e-bikes it often holds the battery |
| Step-through | Frame without a top tube, easy to get on |

## Rail, air and water

| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| Nose / cab end | Front of a train; shape drives identity and aerodynamics |
| Bogie | Wheel set under a rail car |
| Pantograph | Roof arm that collects power from overhead lines |
| Livery | Paint and graphics scheme on a train, bus or aircraft |
| Cabin monument | Fixed cabin element: galley, toilet, bar |
| Sheer line | Profile line of a boat's deck edge |
| Freeboard | Hull height above the waterline |
| Transom | Flat back of a boat's hull |

## Interiors

| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| IP / instrument panel | The dashboard structure |
| Centre console | The unit between front seats |
| H-point | Hip point; sets seating posture and headroom |
| Ingress / egress | Getting in and out |
| Soft-touch zones | Areas hands and elbows touch, given softer materials |
