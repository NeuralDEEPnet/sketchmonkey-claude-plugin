# Review checklist

Go through each lens and note what you actually see. Skip lenses that don't apply.

## 1. Fit to the brief
- Does it do what the brief asks, for the people it names?
- Are the must-haves visible: materials, size cues, required parts?
- Does it feel the way the brief wants (calm, premium, rugged, playful)?

## 2. Proportion and gesture
- Main masses: do the big volumes relate in clear ratios (about 1:1, 1:2, 2:3) or do they fight?
- Visual weight: is it heavy where it should be stable and light where it should feel quick?
- Gesture: one clear direction or attitude, or several competing ones?
- Vehicles: wheelbase, overhangs, wheel size against body, greenhouse against body side, stance.

## 3. Form and surfaces
- Are surfaces taut and intentional, or lumpy and undecided?
- Do highlights run smoothly, without wobbles or sudden breaks?
- Are radii consistent in a family (all tight, all soft, or a clear hierarchy)?
- Do lines start and end somewhere meaningful?

## 4. Details and hierarchy
- Is there one primary detail, a few secondary ones and quiet everything else?
- Do controls, seams, vents and fasteners look placed on purpose?
- Is there stray text, a garbled logo or an invented feature?

## 5. Colour, material and finish
- Is the palette limited and purposeful (usually one main colour, one secondary, one accent)?
- Do material choices suit the use (grip where hands go, durable where it's knocked)?
- Do finishes separate parts clearly (matte against gloss, texture against smooth)?

## 6. Use and ergonomics
- Where do hands, feet or bodies meet it, and do those areas look comfortable and obvious?
- Can a first-time user tell how it works, opens, charges or is cleaned?
- Is scale believable?

## 7. Early manufacturing flags
Name these only as things to check:
- Moulded plastics: sharp internal corners, undercuts that need complex tooling, very thin or very thick walls, no visible place for a parting line.
- Metal: deep draws, tight bends, cosmetic surfaces that will show weld or tool marks.
- Assembly: no visible way to fasten parts, seams across a hero surface, too many separate parts.
- Vehicles: panel gaps that would be hard to hold, glass with extreme curvature, wheel arches that leave no room for steering or suspension travel.

## 8. Story and brand
- Would someone recognise it again from its silhouette or one signature detail?
- Does it sit well beside the person's other products or brand rules?
